## 6.1.1 Video: Network media types

**Network media** = what gives communication a **connection and a path** to travel over in a network.

These days, most connections are either:

- **Wireless**, which goes through the air, so there's no physical media, or
- **Copper**, using cables.

### Twisted pair (UTP)

The most common connection in an **Ethernet** network is **unshielded twisted pair**, or **UTP** for short.

- It's made of a bunch of wires, and in most Ethernet cables that's **eight wires**, arranged in **pairs**.
- Each pair is **color coded**, so the **same pattern** shows up at both ends of the cable.
- The **connectors** are what give the copper connection between the cable and a device, like a **NIC** or a port on a **switch or router**.
- The connectors have to be **seated well** and installed **according to specific standards**.
- The connector is called **RJ45**, and it works for almost all Ethernet cables used today.

### Coaxial cable

Coax is probably familiar because it's how **cable TV** is connected.

- Connectors: a **BNC** connector, or **screw-on F-type** connectors.
- It's different from twisted pair: it has **one solid copper core** running the whole length of the cable, and the communication happens **across that core**.
- Around the core is a **plastic insulating layer**, and around that is a **metal mesh** that shields against **electromagnetic interference**.

### Fiber-optic cable

Fiber is often used for **long distances**. (The video shows a short-distance one that connects networking equipment **inside a building**.)

- Made of **glass or plastic**.
- It has a **solid core**, surrounded by a **second layer of glass or plastic** that acts as a **reflective surface** to carry the **patterns of light**.
- It only carries **light**, and there's **no copper** in it, so **electromagnetic interference doesn't affect it**. That's a big advantage over any copper cable.
- It can carry light signals **much farther**, like **miles**.
- It's more **flexible** than copper, because there are different types of fiber and different devices that create the light signals. So the **speed** each cable supports can vary a lot.

## 6.1.2 Three media types

Data is sent across a network on **media**, which gives the message a **channel** to travel over from source to destination.

Modern networks mainly use **three types** of media:

| Media | How data is sent |
|---|---|
| **Metal wires within cables** (copper) | Data is encoded into **electrical impulses** |
| **Glass or plastic fibers within cables** (fiber-optic) | Data is encoded into **pulses of light** |
| **Wireless transmission** | Data is encoded by **modulating specific frequencies of electromagnetic waves** |

So my guess in the Module 6 README was right: **copper, fiber, and wireless**.

**Connecting the dots:** this is the **encoding** idea from 5.1.2, where bits get turned into sounds, light, or electrical impulses depending on the media. Same thing, now with the names of the three media. "Modulation" is a new word, and the course doesn't explain it here. I'm guessing it's how the wireless signal is changed to carry the bits.

### How to choose the media

There are **four main criteria**:

1. What's the **maximum distance** the media can carry a signal successfully?
2. What's the **environment** where it will be installed?
3. How much **data** needs to be sent, and how **fast**?
4. What's the **cost** of the media and the installation?

**Try it:** here's my attempt at using those criteria (these are my own examples):

| Situation | What I'd pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A desktop in a home office, a few meters from the router | **UTP** (or Wi-Fi) | Short distance, cheap, and it works with Ethernet |
| A link between two buildings far apart | **Fiber** | Long distance, and no interference |
| Mobile devices in a hospital room | **Wireless** | They move around, so cables wouldn't be practical |

## 6.1.3 Common network cables

The **three most common network cables** are **twisted-pair**, **coaxial**, and **fiber-optic**.

### Twisted-pair cable

- **Ethernet** generally uses twisted-pair cables to connect devices. Since Ethernet is the foundation of most local networks, twisted-pair is the **most common** network cable.
- Wires are **grouped in pairs** and **twisted together** to **reduce interference**.
- The pairs are **colored** so I can identify the **same wire at each end**. Usually, in each pair, one wire is a solid color and its partner is the same color **striped on white**.

### Coaxial cable

- One of the **earliest** types of network cabling.
- It's the kind of copper cable used by **cable TV companies**, and also to connect parts of **satellite communication systems**.
- It has a **single rigid copper core** that carries the signal. The core is usually surrounded by **insulation**, **braided metal shielding**, and a **protective jacket**.
- It's used as a **high-frequency transmission line** for **high-frequency or broadband signals**.

### Fiber-optic cable

- **Glass or plastic**, about as thick as a **human hair**.
- Carries digital information at **very high speeds over long distances**.
- It uses **light instead of electricity**, so **electrical interference doesn't affect the signal**.
- It has uses beyond communications, like **medical imaging, medical treatment, and mechanical engineering inspection**.
- **Very high bandwidth**, so it can carry huge amounts of data.
- Used in **backbone networks**, **large enterprise environments**, and **large data centers**, and **extensively by telephone companies**.

### Comparing them

| | **Twisted-pair (UTP)** | **Coaxial** | **Fiber-optic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Signal** | Electrical | Electrical | Light |
| **Structure** | Pairs of wires twisted together (usually 8 wires) | Single rigid copper core, insulation, braided shield, jacket | Glass or plastic core with a reflective outer layer |
| **Connector** | **RJ45** | **BNC** or **F-type** | Not covered |
| **Interference** | Twisting reduces it | Shielding helps | **Not affected** by electrical interference |
| **Where it's used** | Ethernet LANs (most common cable) | Cable TV, satellite systems | Backbones, enterprises, data centers, telephone companies, plus medical and engineering uses |

## What I want to remember

- **Network media** provides the **channel** for communication. Modern networks use three types: **copper** (electrical impulses), **fiber** (pulses of light), and **wireless** (modulated electromagnetic waves).
- **Four criteria** for choosing media: **distance**, **environment**, **amount of data and speed**, and **cost**.
- **UTP**: the most common Ethernet cable. Usually 8 color-coded wires in twisted pairs, with an **RJ45** connector.
- **Coaxial**: a single copper core with insulation, braided shielding, and a jacket. Used for cable TV and satellite systems, with **BNC** or **F-type** connectors.
- **Fiber-optic**: glass or plastic that carries **light**, so it's **immune to electrical interference**. Very high bandwidth and long distance. Used in backbones, data centers, and by telephone companies.
