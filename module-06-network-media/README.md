## 6.0 Introduction

### 6.0.1 Why should I take this module?

Back to Kishori at the hospital, and this time she has a puzzle.

Here's what she knows:

- The **15 to 20 devices** in a patient's room connect to the network **wirelessly**.
- She also uses a **wireless tablet**.
- At the nurses' station, she noticed the **wireless network on her desktop computer was disabled**.
- But she **still has access to patient records**.

So her question is: **how is the desktop connected?**

That leads to the bigger question: **how does communication actually get across a network?** The answer is **network media**. Media gives the message a **channel** to travel over from the **source to the destination**. In modern networks, there are **primarily three types** of media, and the module asks if I know what they are.

**Connecting the dots:**

- I think I already know these from earlier: in 1.2.3, signals traveled over **copper wire** (electrical), **fiber-optic cable** (light), or **through the air** (wireless). In 4.2.2, I saw the actual cable names: **UTP**, **coaxial**, and **fiber**. So my guess for the three types is **copper, fiber, and wireless**.
- Kishori's desktop has wireless disabled but still works, so my guess is that it's using a **wired connection**, probably an **Ethernet cable** to the network. That would also fit the Module 2 question about how her laptop notes show up on the desktop at the nursing station.
- And **network media** was the third category of network infrastructure back in 2.2.2, next to **end devices** and **intermediate devices**. So this module is about the third piece.
- I wonder if those 15 to 20 devices in the patient's room are the connected medical devices from 1.1.7 (hospital monitors and such). That's just a guess.
