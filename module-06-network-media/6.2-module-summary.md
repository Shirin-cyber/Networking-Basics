## 6.1 Network Media Types

### The three types of media

| Media | How data is sent |
|---|---|
| **Copper** (metal wires in cables) | Encoded into **electrical impulses** |
| **Fiber-optic** (glass or plastic fibers in cables) | Encoded into **pulses of light** |
| **Wireless** | Encoded by **modulating specific frequencies of electromagnetic waves** |

### How to choose

The four criteria are **distance**, **environment**, **amount of data and speed**, and **cost** (of the media and the installation).

### The three common cables

| | **Twisted-pair (UTP)** | **Coaxial** | **Fiber-optic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Signal** | Electrical | Electrical | Light |
| **Structure** | Color-coded pairs of wires twisted together (usually 8 wires) | Single rigid copper core, insulation, braided shield, jacket | Glass or plastic core with a reflective outer layer |
| **Connector** | **RJ45** | **BNC** or **F-type** | Not covered |
| **Interference** | Twisting reduces it | Shielding helps | **Not affected** by electrical interference |
| **Used for** | Ethernet LANs (the most common cable) | Cable TV, satellite systems | Backbones, data centers, enterprises, telephone companies, medical uses |

Other key points:

- **UTP** is the most common because **Ethernet** is the foundation of most local networks.
- **Fiber** has very high bandwidth and can go very far, and since it uses light, **electrical interference doesn't affect it**.
- Connectors have to be **installed according to standards** so devices can work together.

## Key terms cheat sheet

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Network media** | The channel a message travels over from source to destination |
| **UTP** | Unshielded twisted pair, the most common Ethernet cable |
| **RJ45** | The connector used on almost all Ethernet cables |
| **Coaxial cable** | A cable with a single copper core and shielding, used for cable TV |
| **BNC / F-type** | Connector types used with coaxial cable |
| **Fiber-optic cable** | Glass or plastic cable that carries light |
| **Electromagnetic interference** | Electrical noise that can disturb signals on copper cable |
| **Modulation** | How wireless signals are encoded onto electromagnetic waves (the course doesn't explain it in detail) |
