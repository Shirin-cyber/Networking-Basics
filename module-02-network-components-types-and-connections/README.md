## 2.0 Introduction

### 2.0.1 Why should I take this module?

Webster's back, and so is **Kishori**. This time her story is about the stuff that makes a network actually work.

Right now, Kishori doesn't really get the roles that different devices play in a network. That includes **end devices**, **intermediate devices**, and **network media**. She started her nursing career writing patient notes in a paper notebook, so all of this is pretty new to her.

Here's where she's comfortable and where she isn't:

| | What she knows |
|---|---|
| **At home** | She has a laptop, a smartphone, and a tablet. Those are **end devices** (also called **hosts**), so that's what she knows best. She also knows they somehow connect to the internet through "that box in the corner of her living room." |
| **At work** | Laptop, desktop, printer, and network-connected hospital equipment |

**Connecting the dots:** back in 1.1 I guessed that "hosts" just means devices on the network. Turns out that's pretty close, since end devices and hosts are the same thing here. I'm also guessing the "box in the corner" is a router or a modem, but I'll find out soon.

### The question that makes it click

Kishori finishes with a patient, leaves the room, and sets down her laptop. Then she goes to the desktop computer at the nursing station to keep working. And she wonders:

- How did the electronic notes she just typed on the laptop show up on the patient's record on the desktop?
- How are those two computers connected?
- How does the computer reach the internet in the first place?

Those are exactly the questions I'd have, so that's what this module is for.
